# POLARIS · Phase I proposal

**Funding requested:** US$10,000 in direct costs over six months.

**Need.** Infrequent site visits can miss brief water-quality changes at remote Arctic sites. Continuous sensors can record when a change occurred, while a retained water sample provides material for a separate laboratory measurement. The first use case is paired baseline and event sampling of turbidity excursions at accessible nearshore research sites.

**Approach.** Build a stationary, supervised two-cartridge tank prototype using a microcontroller, turbidity and temperature sensors, flow measurement, and a replaceable fluid path. Compare event-triggered sampling with scheduled-only sampling under matched cartridge budgets. Compare collected volumes and sample measurements with calibrated references and manual grab samples. Use controlled cold and fault tests to establish where the prototype can operate reliably.

**Deliverables.** A bench prototype, an itemized build record, repeatable test protocols and results, a mission database with calibration and custody records, and a go/no-go report for a supervised nearshore pilot. The project currently has a design and synthetic data demonstration.

**Success criteria.** Proposed gates include at least 90% event capture in 20 controlled trials and collected volume within ±10% of target in at least 18 of 20 normal draws. Evaluators will also receive failure counts, measured power use, blank and carryover results, and cost per usable sample. The program reserves field deployment for a separately scoped phase with local collaboration and review.

**Use of funds.** $1,000 prototype hardware and supplies, $4,500 engineering labor, $3,700 validation and partner planning, and $800 reserve. Affordability will be evaluated using actual labor, consumables, servicing, recovery and sample acceptance, alongside the hardware cost.
