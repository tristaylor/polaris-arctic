# Field governance and risks

Phase I work is supervised in a controlled facility. Before selecting a field site, identify the land and water managers, applicable Tribal or Indigenous authorities, and institutional safety lead. Resolve permissions for access, instrument placement, water collection, retrieval, sample transfer and transport with the responsible authorities. Review chemical, battery and shipping requirements with the institution and carrier. Obtain applicable determinations before mobilization.

Engage local partners while defining questions and site selection, agree compensation and responsibilities, and jointly decide access, coordinates, custody, attribution, release timing and return of results. Keep sensitive coordinates and restricted datasets out of the public repository. Use the [IARPC SHARE principles](https://www.iarpccollaborations.org/principles.html) and [NSF Arctic engagement guidance](https://www.nsf.gov/od/opp/arc/arctic-community-engagement) as planning references.

| Hazard | Control and decision owner |
|---|---|
| Ice, weather, tides, wildlife and unsafe access | Field lead sets travel windows, stop criteria, communication and emergency plans with local partners. Start from a reachable shore site. |
| Device loss, entanglement or spill | Field lead approves attachment and retrieval plan, tracks inventory and arranges safe recovery and waste removal. |
| Freezing, ingress or electrical fault | Engineering lead uses monitored limits, isolation, protective power and fail-closed actuation. |
| Contamination, custody gaps or invalid calibration | Science lead checks blanks, controls and calibration, quarantines suspect samples and retains audit records. |
| Restricted site or sample information | Data steward applies agreements and reviews public releases. |
